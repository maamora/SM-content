# STUDIO creative direction

## Chosen direction: The composed creative desk

STUDIO is a quiet creative instrument for teams that want more point of view, not more interface. The experience keeps the editorial graphite, paper, and acid-lime foundation of the earlier STUDIO build, then makes the central workflow feel more authored: a brief enters a composed visual desk, references and outputs sit together, language remains attached to the image, and the next action is obvious.

This direction is informed by Flora's emphasis on creative control, techniques, iteration, and visual context. It is intentionally **not** a literal infinite-canvas clone and avoids turning the product into a decorative graph.

## Principles

1. **Compose, do not clutter.** Use one dominant creative stage with a clear brief and a compact inspector instead of a field of disconnected cards.
2. **Keep context attached.** Prompts, tone, format, language, and approval state should remain close to the visual output they describe.
3. **Make the next move legible.** Every surface should offer one primary action: open the desk, continue composing, approve, export, or publish.
4. **Let motion support focus.** Use restrained reveal, hover lift, and progress cues; never animate the whole interface into noise.

## Visual system

- **Graphite** is the working surface: focused, cinematic, and calm.
- **Paper** is the thinking surface: readable, warm, and human.
- **Lime** is the moment of movement: an active state, not a decorative gradient.
- **Serif display type** carries the point of view; compact sans-serif metadata keeps the tool precise.
- **Sharp frames** and small offset shadows make the work feel pinned to a desk rather than floating in a generic dashboard.

## Content voice

Headlines should be short, specific, and slightly editorial. CTAs should describe movement rather than administration: “Open the desk,” “Keep composing,” “Make it ready,” and “Move it forward.” Empty states should explain the next real backend-backed action instead of pretending that data exists.
