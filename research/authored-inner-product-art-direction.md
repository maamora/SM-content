# Authored inner-product art direction

## Why the current system still reads as generic

The existing inner product is clean, but it repeats familiar generated-dashboard signals: a uniform dark rail, oversized serif statements on every route, lime accents applied in several unrelated places, evenly divided panels, and a composed-but-safe warm-paper canvas. The problem is not the palette; it is the lack of a single visual metaphor that governs layout, states, and detail.

The research reinforces this diagnosis. Art direction is the deliberate selection of typography, words, layout, colour, and images to communicate a precise meaning—not a collection of individually tasteful components.[1] Strong editorial work uses hierarchy, margin, rhythm, and restraint to make information readable rather than decorative.[2]

## Three directions considered

| Direction | Intent | Probability |
|---|---|---:|
| **The Ink Room** | A high-contrast print-production studio: proof marks, registration lines, raw paper, and black ink. | 0.06 |
| **Quiet Institution** | A slow cultural archive: luminous paper, gallery labels, restrained type, and a deep olive index. | 0.04 |
| **Signal Press** | A contemporary independent magazine desk: warm stock, black type, acid signal tape, oversized crop marks, and operational annotations. | 0.08 |

## Selected direction: Signal Press

> **STUDIO is a creative operating desk for teams that want a point of view before a post.**

Signal Press combines the existing black, paper, sage, and acid-lime palette with the visual vocabulary of a contemporary editorial studio. It treats every screen as a working proof: one dominant idea, one active hand-off, a measured index, and details that feel physical rather than digital decoration.

### Core principles

1. **Proof, not dashboard.** Surfaces use crop marks, registration guides, margins, and editorial labels only where they clarify the work.
2. **One commanding gesture per screen.** A route gets one oversized composition, one primary action, and one reserved lime signal.
3. **Asymmetry with purpose.** Rails behave as annotated margins; content starts and stops at deliberately offset edges instead of settling into generic card grids.
4. **Material contrast.** Graphite means active production, raw paper means records and viewing, pale sage means context, and lime means a committed next step.

### Signature elements

The inner product will use narrow **registration rules** (two black lines plus a lime tick), **numbered proof labels** in monospace, and **paper-shift blocks** that look slightly offset rather than perfectly rounded cards. Micro-motion remains short: paper blocks lift by 2–3 pixels on hover; lime ticks travel a few pixels on state change; all nonessential movement respects reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography and voice

Display typography is used for one decisive line only, with tightly cropped proportions and a confident editorial rhythm. Utilities, records, and state sit in compact monospace labels. Headlines sound like an art director’s instruction, not SaaS filler: “**Hold the source until the point of view is clear.**” and “**Make the post earn its place in the feed.**”
